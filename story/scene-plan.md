# The World Through My Eyes — scene plan

Target runtime: 3–5 minutes. The narration can be shortened or expanded during editing.

## Character continuity lock
Use the same 11-year-old boy in every scene: tousled brown hair, freckles, large expressive brown eyes, black hoodie, blue jeans. Match the provided bedroom reference's cinematic, detailed 3D animated look: soft realistic textures, expressive but natural faces, warm cinematic lighting, rich environments. Do not change his age, face, hair, eye color, or core outfit between scenes.

Dante is his Boxer dog and should look like the same dog in every appearance. Add a dedicated approved Dante reference image before generating scenes if possible. Keep the chickens consistent as a small backyard flock.

## Scene 1 — A mind full of questions
**Visual:** Boy in his bedroom, surrounded by books, models, collections, and objects connected to his interests. He studies a fascinating fact in a book or on a screen.
**Narration:** “Meet a very curious eleven-year-old. His mind is always discovering something new. He loves facts—amazing facts, unusual facts, and little details other people might miss. When something catches his attention, he wants to learn everything about it.”

## Scene 2 — His happy place
**Visual:** He visits the chicken coop, checks food and water, and watches his chickens explore.
**Narration:** “One of his happiest places is outside with his chickens. He notices their different habits and enjoys caring for them. His gentle heart shows in the way he looks after the animals he loves.”

## Scene 3 — Meet Dante
**Visual:** Dante, his Boxer, greets him in the yard. The boy kneels to pet him and they spend a quiet moment together.
**Narration:** “And then there is Dante, his Boxer and loyal companion. Dante is always glad to see him. Together they can enjoy a moment without needing to explain a thing.”

## Scene 4 — Finding connections
**Visual:** At school, classmates chat about shared interests. The boy tries to share an interesting fact, but the conversation moves on. Show his wish to connect without portraying classmates as cruel.
**Narration:** “Making connections with other kids can sometimes be hard. He may be excited about a subject that other children do not know much about. He wants to share what he loves, and he wants to feel included. Finding common ground can take time.”

## Scene 5 — What people may not see
**Visual:** A sequence of thoughtful moments: the boy concentrating, being kind, learning, and taking a quiet break when the day feels overwhelming.
**Narration:** “People may notice that he communicates or connects in his own way. They may not always see his curiosity, kindness, humor, and the effort he puts into navigating the world. Autism is one part of who he is. It does not tell his whole story.”

## Scene 6 — A friendship begins
**Visual:** A classmate asks him about a topic he enjoys. They listen to each other and discover a shared interest.
**Narration:** “A connection can begin when someone slows down, listens, and shows genuine interest. Friendship does not mean liking all the same things. It means making room for each other.”

## Scene 7 — The world through his eyes
**Visual:** Golden afternoon in the backyard. The boy walks with Dante while the chickens roam nearby. End on a warm, confident portrait of the boy.
**Narration:** “He is eleven, with a mind full of facts and a heart full of kindness. He has chickens to care for, Dante by his side, and so much more to discover. He does not need to be like everyone else to belong. He deserves to be understood, included, and celebrated just as he is.”

## End card
**THE WORLD THROUGH MY EYES**  
*Different doesn't mean less. Understanding begins when we take the time to listen.*
